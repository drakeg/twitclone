# SMB pilot outreach and qualification kit

This document supports real discovery conversations for the Ripple member-community
pilot. It is a reusable public-safe template only. It contains no prospect/customer
records and does not claim market validation.

## Who to contact first

Prioritize organizations that are plausible matches for the current pilot:

- roughly 25–500 members;
- one or a few staff/volunteers handling member/community administration;
- active member communication or community discussion;
- evidence of manual workflows, fragmented tools, or repetitive administration;
- no obvious requirement for enterprise-scale integrations before a pilot can start.

Examples include local professional associations, hobby/enthusiast clubs,
nonprofits with active member communities, coaching/mastermind groups, alumni or
volunteer communities, and small trade/member organizations.

Avoid treating organization size alone as qualification. The strongest signal is
a repeated administrative problem that consumes measurable time or money.

## Public research checklist

Before contacting a prospect, use only public information to answer:

- What does the organization do?
- Approximately how large or active does the member community appear?
- What public community/member tools are visible?
- Is there a clear person/role responsible for membership, operations, community,
  programs, or communications?
- Is there a plausible workflow Ripple could improve?

Do not copy personal/private data into this repository. Store real prospect notes
in an approved private system.

## Initial outreach template

Keep the first message short and discovery-oriented. Do not lead with a long
feature list.

> Hi [Name] — I’m testing a lightweight member-community platform for smaller
> organizations that currently juggle community discussion, member roles, and
> admin work across multiple tools. I’m looking for a few people who manage a
> real member community to show me how they handle that work today. Would you be
> open to a 15-minute conversation and a short demo? I’m specifically trying to
> learn what is still manual or frustrating, not sell you a finished product.

Do not claim customers, savings, adoption, or validation that does not exist.

## Follow-up when there is interest

Confirm that the prospect has a relevant current workflow before scheduling a
demo:

1. Roughly how many members do you support?
2. Which tools do you use for member records, discussion/community, moderation,
   and communications?
3. What work still requires spreadsheets, copy/paste, manual reminders, or
   duplicate entry?
4. Who performs that work and approximately how often?
5. Is improving that workflow a current priority?

If there is no recurring pain, do not force-fit the prospect to Ripple.

## 15-minute discovery/demo agenda

**Minutes 0–5 — Current workflow**

- Ask how the organization manages members and community activity today.
- Identify the most repetitive or fragmented task.
- Quantify time/cost when the prospect can reasonably estimate it.

**Minutes 5–10 — Focused Ripple demo**

Show only capabilities that map to the stated pain:

- Space owner/moderator/member roles;
- operator dashboard;
- recent membership/activity context;
- moderation/appeals;
- conversations/resources/messages when relevant.

Do not present descriptive counts as engagement scores or business outcomes.

**Minutes 10–15 — Pilot decision**

Ask:

- What would still block a real pilot?
- What data would need to move?
- Which integration is genuinely mandatory?
- Would a hosted pilot be worth trying?
- How does the current $49–$79/month hypothesis compare with the problem solved?
- What concrete next step would make sense?

## Qualification scorecard

Use this scorecard privately for consistency. Do not treat it as scientific
market validation.

| Signal | 0 | 1 | 2 |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Repeated admin pain | None | Occasional | Frequent/measurable |
| Tool fragmentation | Single adequate tool | Some workarounds | Multiple tools/manual handoffs |
| Decision access | Unknown | Influencer | Buyer/owner involved |
| Pilot urgency | No priority | Someday | Current/near-term |
| Price relevance | No budget/problem value | Unclear | Existing spend or clear time-cost |
| MVP fit | Major missing platform | Some gaps | Current pilot solves core pain |

A score does not replace judgment. Strong prospects usually have a clear,
repeated pain plus willingness to take a concrete next step.

## Evidence categories

After each conversation, privately record only what the prospect actually said
or demonstrated:

- current workflow;
- current tools;
- repeated pain;
- approximate admin time/cost if volunteered;
- must-have blocker;
- pricing reaction;
- requested next step;
- pilot/payment commitment status.

Separate **observed facts** from **interpretation** and **future ideas**.

## Build/no-build rule

Do not create a feature because one prospect casually requests it.

Promote a request into the public backlog when at least one of these is true:

- multiple qualified prospects report the same blocker;
- a committed pilot is blocked by a small, reusable capability;
- the capability materially improves the target SMB workflow and fits the
  existing product architecture.

Document the evidence category without placing private prospect information in
the repository.

## Current validation threshold

The working decision gate remains:

- 5 qualified discovery conversations;
- 3 prospects confirming the same or closely related administrative pain;
- 1 concrete pilot or paid next step.

Until those conditions are met, describe Ripple's member-community opportunity
as a hypothesis, not validated product-market fit.
